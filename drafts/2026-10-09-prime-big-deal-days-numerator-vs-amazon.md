## LINKEDIN POST

Amazon and a consumer data firm just told two completely different stories about the same 48 hours.

Amazon says customers shopped millions of items at the lowest prices of the year. A consumer panel, Numerator, says average household spend fell 22% on day one of Prime Big Deal Days — from $106.41 last year to $86.98 this year.

Amazon's response: the estimate is "wholly inaccurate," based on fewer than 200 verified buyers out of tens of millions of shoppers.

Both of those things can be true at once. A panel of a few hundred households is a thin sample. A company's own press line isn't independent data either. Neither number is the one that should change how you plan Q4.

The number buried further down in the same Numerator report is more useful than the headline fight:

→ Average order size rose 5%, to $51.03.

→ 39% of households that shopped the event placed two or more separate orders.

→ The best-selling first-day items were almost entirely sub-$20 consumables — protein shakes, toilet paper, cleaning wipes.

Read together, that's not "shoppers spent less." It's shoppers splitting the same spend across more, smaller baskets — stocking up on staples instead of making one big discretionary purchase.

That cuts differently depending on what you sell.

If you're in consumables or everyday staples, that behavior is a demand signal in your favor. The event is functioning as a restock trigger, not a splurge trigger.

If you sell considered, higher-ticket discretionary products, the lesson isn't about the discount depth. It's about whether you were visible enough across the smaller, more frequent search sessions that behavior implies.

Either way — the panel number and Amazon's rebuttal are both proxies. The only dataset that tells you what actually happened to your catalog is sitting in your own Business Reports right now: orders, AOV, and sessions, day by day, against last year's event.

Pull that before you build your Black Friday assumptions on someone else's headline.

What did your AOV and order count actually do this week? Comment or send me your top ASINs and I'll help you read what moved.
