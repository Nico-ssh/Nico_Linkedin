## LINKEDIN POST

Your Sponsored Products campaigns started spending outside Amazon on August 10.

No email that flagged it as urgent. No checkbox you had to tick. It just started serving.

Here's what happened:

Amazon auto-enrolled every Sponsored Products campaign into off-Amazon placements — your ads now show up inside creator content through the Amazon Influencer Programme, on top of the usual on-Amazon spots. Your existing bid and budget carried over unchanged. Nobody touched a setting, and where your ad dollars go changed anyway.

Three mechanics worth knowing before you decide anything:

→ The control lives under "Settings for ads served off Amazon," at the campaign level. Two options: "Increase reach" (the default) or "Limit off-Amazon spend." You can switch mid-flight, and Amazon added a bulksheet column for it if you're managing this across a large portfolio.

→ Your max bid applies to every click, on or off Amazon. But bid adjustments for Top of Search and Product Pages don't carry over to off-Amazon placements — only dynamic bidding does.

→ Off-Amazon clicks bill at the same per-click price, from the same campaign budget. It's not a separate line item you'd notice in a routine budget review.

None of this is good or bad by default. Off-Amazon reach can be real incremental demand for some catalogs. For others, it quietly pulls budget away from placements that were already converting.

The actual problem isn't the feature. It's finding out about it a month from now, after it's already shaped a month of data.

What I'd check this week if this were my account:

1. Pull campaign settings and confirm which option each campaign is on — check per campaign, not account-wide.
2. Managing a large portfolio? Use the off-Amazon column in Sponsored Products bulksheets to review and adjust at scale instead of one campaign at a time.
3. Segment on-Amazon vs. off-Amazon performance in your reporting before drawing any conclusion about "PPC efficiency" this month.
4. Re-check your Top of Search and Product Page bid adjustments — they're not doing what you think on off-Amazon placements.

Default settings are never neutral. Somebody always benefits from the fact that most advertisers never open that menu.

If you run Amazon Ads for your own brand or a client's, go check that setting before you review this month's numbers.
