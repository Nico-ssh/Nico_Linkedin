## LINKEDIN POST

On September 30, Amazon starts enforcing a delivery metric most FBM sellers have never opened.

It's called Business Hour Delivery Rate. If you run seller-fulfilled offers and sell to Amazon Business customers, it can turn off that entire revenue stream in one month.

Here's what it actually measures:

The percentage of your seller-fulfilled shipments that arrive at Amazon Business customers during their stated operating hours — tracked on a rolling 14-day window.

Not "delivered on time." Delivered while the business was actually open to receive it.

Three things worth knowing before the 30th:

→ The bar is 90%. Fall below it on September 30, and Amazon sends a notification with recommendations — not a deactivation yet.

→ The real deadline is October 30. Still under 90% by then, and your seller-fulfilled offers get deactivated specifically for Amazon Business buyers. Your FBA listings and regular retail offers aren't touched.

→ This isn't always about how fast you ship. A business address that's technically also a residence, stale operating-hours data on file, or a carrier that completes the drop-off after the listed closing time can all drag the rate down even when you shipped on schedule.

Amazon has pointed to three settings that, together, are built to keep shipments inside this window: Automated Handling Time, Shipping Settings Automation, and Amazon Buy Shipping. Turning all three on is currently the most direct fix sellers are reporting.

This only applies in the US, UK and Germany for now, and only to seller-fulfilled orders sold to Amazon Business accounts — not your whole catalog, not FBA.

But "not my whole account" is exactly the framing that makes people skip checking it until the offers are already off.

What I'd check this week if this were my account:

1. Pull your current Business Hour Delivery Rate in Seller Central — most sellers have never looked at this report.
2. Confirm the operating hours on file for your top Amazon Business buyers are actually accurate.
3. Check whether Automated Handling Time and Shipping Settings Automation are already turned on — if not, turn them on before the 30th.
4. If you're near or under 90%, model what losing Amazon Business orders would do to FBM revenue before Amazon does it for you.

A metric named "Business Hour Delivery Rate" doesn't sound like something that can shut off a channel.

That's exactly why most sellers won't check it until it already has.

If you run seller-fulfilled offers and sell to Amazon Business accounts, this is worth ten minutes before the 30th — not a Plan of Action after it.
