## LINKEDIN POST

An authenticity complaint on one listing can now take down your whole Amazon account. Not just the listing.

Not the account.

That's the shift enforcement specialists and appeal attorneys are flagging through 2025 into 2026: complaints that used to stay ASIN-level are escalating to full account deactivation more often — especially once a brand gets flagged across multiple ASINs, or an authenticity issue lands on top of other account health flags already sitting quietly in the background.

Here's the part that catches good sellers off guard: a brand owner can file an inauthentic complaint without ever buying your product. Amazon doesn't require a purchase to open the case. It requires you to prove your sourcing is clean — fast, after the fact.

If your invoices aren't ready when that happens, you're appealing from a losing position.

What "ready" actually looks like, based on what's working in real appeals right now:

→ Your invoices name a distributor actually authorized by the brand — listed on the brand's own site or in brand registry, not just "a supplier who had stock."

→ Each invoice ties a specific quantity to what you've actually sold, within the trailing 12 months. Volume gets cross-checked, not just document formatting.

→ You're not assembling this after a complaint lands. A quarterly pass — matching units sold to units purchased — is far cheaper than an emergency Plan of Action written against a 48-hour clock.

→ Your listing photography still matches current pack shots. "This doesn't look genuine" is an easier complaint to file, and a harder one to defend, when your images are stale.

Most sellers losing accounts over this aren't counterfeiters. They're legitimate sellers who couldn't produce, in 48 hours, what should have already been sitting in a folder.

Order Defect Rate gets most of the fear. Sourcing documentation is what's actually taking accounts down account-wide right now.

If you sell branded or name-brand-adjacent products and haven't looked at your invoice file in a few months, that's twenty minutes worth spending this week — before a complaint turns it into a 48-hour scramble.
