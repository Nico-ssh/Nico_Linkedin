## LINKEDIN POST

In 3 days, Amazon adds a fee to every unit you ship through FBA.

That's not the one that's going to hurt your Q4 margin the most.

Starting October 15, the holiday peak fulfillment fee kicks in — the same seasonal bump as last year, averaging around $0.32 per unit on top of your normal FBA fee, running through January 14. Every seller watching their fee dashboard already knows this one is coming.

Here's what most sellers aren't stacking on top of it:

The 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge Amazon added to FBA fulfillment fees back in April is still running. It never got a confirmed end date. It's calculated on your fulfillment fee, not your sale price — and it doesn't pause for peak season. It compounds with it.

Standard-size storage jumps too. Amazon's own pattern, year after year: roughly $0.78 per cubic foot January through September, up to about $2.40 per cubic foot October through December. If you're still sitting on inventory in a fulfillment center past October 1, that line alone can outweigh the other two combined.

And the detail that catches sellers every single year: the peak fee is charged by ship date, not order date. An order placed October 14 but shipped October 15 pays the peak rate. Amazon has applied this exact timing rule for at least three years running.

None of these three show up in your ACOS. They show up in your margin — only once you actually recalculate it.

The break-even ACOS you built your Q3 budgets on is already wrong for Q4. Three fee lines just stacked on top of your landed cost, and the only notice most sellers get is a smaller deposit next month.

I put together the 3-step recalculation I run on client accounts before Black Friday budgets lock. Swipe through.

Already run this on your own numbers? Drop how much your break-even ACOS moved in the comments — curious how much it's shifting by category this year.
