## LINKEDIN POST

Two days ago, Amazon quietly deleted the excuse "I barely sell that ASIN."

If you list in certain categories, you now need $1,000,000 in liability insurance — even if that listing did $40 in sales last month.

Here's what changed on November 2:

Before: if your monthly sales on a listing stayed under $10,000, you had breathing room. Insurance only kicked in once you crossed that line, with 30 days to get a certificate on file.

Now: for Amazon's "enhanced safety" categories — children's products, cosmetic/ingestible products, and lithium battery products are the examples showing up consistently across seller reports — that sales-volume shield is gone. The requirement applies at any volume. New listing, old listing, one sale or one thousand: same $1M bar.

The part that catches sellers off guard: this isn't a future deadline. It's already live. Sellers in Amazon's forums are asking right now why they're suddenly being asked for proof of coverage on listings they assumed were too small to matter.

If you sell outside these categories, the standard $10,000/month + 30-day rule still appears to apply as before — this isn't a blanket change for every seller. But "I don't think I'm affected" is not the same as confirming it.

Four things to do this week:

→ Check Seller Central: Settings → Account Info → Business Insurance. See what it says about your account right now.

→ Pull your full catalog and flag anything in a safety-sensitive category, even low-volume or dormant listings.

→ If flagged, get a certificate naming Amazon as additional insured, written on an occurrence basis, at $1M per occurrence/aggregate — before you get a deactivation notice instead of a request.

→ Don't assume your current broker's standard e-commerce policy already satisfies this. Confirm the limits and the additional-insured language explicitly.

Account health in 2026 isn't just inventory and feedback scores anymore. It's paperwork most sellers haven't looked at since they signed up.

The sellers who get caught by this won't be the ones breaking rules. They'll be the ones who never checked.
