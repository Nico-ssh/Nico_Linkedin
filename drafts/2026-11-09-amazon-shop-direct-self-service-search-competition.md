## LINKEDIN POST

Amazon's search results have started including stores that don't sell on Amazon at all.

The program isn't new. What changed on November 4 is that it stopped being invitation-only.

Amazon rolled out a self-service "Merchant Workspace" for its Shop Direct program. Any eligible online retailer — a brand with its own site, not a seller on Amazon's marketplace — can now register directly, submit a product feed, and show up in US Amazon search and in Alexa for Shopping. No referral fee. No Buy Box to win, because they're not in that competition at all.

Shoppers who tap a "Shop Direct" listing either land on the merchant's own site, or check out inside Amazon through its "Buy for Me" agent, without leaving the app.

Here's what Amazon hasn't said:

→ Whether Shop Direct listings can ever carry Sponsored Products or Sponsored Brands placements
→ How they rank against marketplace offers in the same search results
→ How a "Buy for Me" purchase gets measured or attributed back to the retailer

Three open questions, not three facts. I'd verify in your own category before drawing any conclusion about your ad spend.

What I'd actually do this week if I managed your account: search your top 10 converting keywords and scroll page one. If a "Shop Direct" label shows up anywhere near your listings, that's new real estate your Sponsored Products budget is now competing with — whether or not you can outbid it.

Your share-of-search used to be contested only by sellers paying the same referral fee you do. As of this week, in some categories, it may also be contested by retailers paying Amazon nothing to be there.

I'm tracking which categories actually show Shop Direct listings this quarter. If you've spotted one competing with your ASINs, drop a comment — happy to take a look and tell you what I'm seeing in other categories.
