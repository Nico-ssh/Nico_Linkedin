## LINKEDIN POST

Two sellers can post the exact same ACOS in November. One is comfortably profitable. The other is funding "growth" out of margin it doesn't have.

ACOS can't tell them apart. It was never built to.

Here's what makes that gap expensive: CPCs climb every Q4. The benchmark sources disagree hard on how much — estimates range from a mild double-digit bump to a lot more, depending who you ask and which category you're in. That disagreement is itself the point: nobody outside your own account actually knows your number. You have to calculate it.

Most sellers watch ACOS through the Q4 spike and assume they're fine, because ACOS only answers one question: did this campaign convert efficiently? It says nothing about whether the business is still making money once you zoom out.

There's a third number that actually answers that, and almost nobody calculates it by SKU: break-even ACOS — the exact ACOS where a campaign stops adding profit and starts just moving inventory at cost.

Above your break-even ACOS, "efficient" and "profitable" stop being the same word.

I put together the 3-number framework I run with clients before Q4 budgets lock — ACOS, TACOS, and break-even ACOS, and what each one is actually telling you. Swipe through.

If you've never calculated break-even ACOS on your top SKUs, this is the week to do it. Bidding only gets more expensive from here.

Calculated yours already? Drop your break-even ACOS range in the comments — curious how much it's actually varying by category this year.
