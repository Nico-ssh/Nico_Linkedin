## LINKEDIN POST

Prime Big Deal Days starts in about 10 hours.

The thing that's going to cost you the most money tomorrow has nothing to do with your bids.

Every year, the same pattern repeats: a campaign's daily budget runs out by early-to-mid afternoon on the peak day — right as the evening hours, historically the highest-converting window of the whole event, are about to start. The bid wasn't the problem. The budget cap hit first, and the campaign went dark for the rest of the day.

Amazon spends fast once competition spikes. Without any pacing on your end, a daily budget that normally lasts 24 hours can evaporate by lunchtime.

What to actually check tonight, before midnight PT:

→ Raise event-day budgets on the campaigns tied to your deal ASINs specifically. Most guidance clusters around 2-3x your normal daily spend for the event days themselves — and a concentrated increase on your best sellers beats a flat bump across the whole account.

→ If your tools support dayparting or hour-level budget rules, turn them on. The goal isn't to spend less. It's to stop the budget from running out before evening traffic even shows up.

→ Check your dynamic bidding setting. "Up and down" lets Amazon raise your bid automatically when competition spikes — useful for visibility, but it can also push your CPC past what you'd set manually. If you want a hard ceiling on cost-per-click through the event, "down only" keeps Amazon from ever bidding above your max.

→ Don't hold event-day campaigns to your normal ACOS ceiling. A deal-day ACOS isn't your real unit economics. It's buying rank and review velocity that typically carries into the following week or two.

→ Treat your daily budget number as a monthly guardrail, not an hourly leash. Amazon's own pacing system already lets a strong campaign spend meaningfully past its cap on a single high-intent day and balance it out over the rest of the month — the number you set back in June isn't necessarily the right one for October 6th. Worth checking your account's current settings rather than assuming.

The accounts that come out of this ahead aren't the ones with the highest bids. They're the ones whose best campaigns were still live at 9pm.

If you're looking at your dashboard tonight and you're not sure your budgets are built to survive the whole day — that's worth fixing before midnight, not after.
