## LINKEDIN POST

Amazon's ad revenue growth got cut in half last quarter.

Most sellers will read that as "ads are getting cheaper." They're reading it backwards.

Here's what the numbers actually say:

Q2 2026: Amazon ad revenue hit $19.8B, up 26% year over year — the fastest pace it had posted in years.

Q3 2026 (reported Oct 29): $19.6B, up roughly 11–12% YoY. Below Wall Street's estimate. Amazon's first advertising "miss" in seven quarters.

Cue the headlines: "Amazon advertising is slowing down."

Except that's not really what happened. Amazon's own explanation: Prime Day sat inside Q2 this year. In 2025, it sat inside Q3. That alone flattered the Q2 comp and flattened the Q3 one. It's a calendar effect, not a drop in demand or a softer auction.

Here's the number that should actually change your plan: Amazon's own guidance for Q4 2026 is growth back up into the high teens year over year.

That's the one that matters to your budget right now. Not the Q3 "miss."

High-teens growth, landing in the exact quarter that holds Black Friday and Cyber Monday, means more advertisers chasing the same placements. Not fewer.

If the Q3 slowdown is your reason for holding Q4 ad spend flat — or trimming it — you're planning off the wrong number.

What I'd actually watch in your own account instead of an earnings headline:

→ Impression share on your top ASINs, week over week
→ CPC trend day-by-day as Q4 opens — not quarter-over-quarter
→ TACOS, not ACOS, since that's the number that tells you if the growth is actually profitable

Earnings headlines are written for investors. Your Q4 budget should be built from your own account's numbers, not theirs.

Drop a comment if you want a second set of eyes on your Q4 pacing before BFCM hits.
