## LINKEDIN POST

Amazon is suing an AI company to keep its shopping agent off Amazon.com.

Walmart just built a front door for AI agents and handed every one of them a key.

Same month. Opposite bets.

Quick recap of where this actually stands:

Amazon has spent a year in court trying to stop Perplexity's Comet browser from buying things on Amazon for users. A judge blocked Comet with an injunction in March. An appeals court vacated that injunction in August. Amazon filed an amended complaint in September. Still unresolved.

Around the same window, Amazon also blocked Meta's shopping assistant, Muse, from shopping on its site.

Then on October 21, Walmart went the other direction: an MCP-based "Agent Gateway" any AI agent can connect to, spending limits and purchase approvals built in, open to any Walmart account — no subscription required.

Amazon isn't anti-agent. It's anti-agent-it-doesn't-own. Its own assistant already buys from other brands' sites on a shopper's behalf through a feature called "Buy for Me." Amazon wants to be the agent standing between your customer and the buy button — not get routed around by someone else's.

Walmart is betting the opposite: open the gate first, while Amazon is still in court, and own the layer where the next wave of purchases might start.

Here's the part operators are missing while everyone argues about who "wins":

A Walmart marketplace executive already said the quiet part out loud — accurate product data, competitive pricing, real-time availability and delivery promises you can actually keep will matter more once an agent is the one doing the shopping.

Not a sharper ad. Not a higher bid. Clean data an algorithm can read without a human checking it twice.

If your listings, pricing sync and inventory feed aren't tight across Amazon Seller Central and Walmart Marketplace right now, you're not ready for a buyer who never sees your product page.

Walmart's gateway opens in weeks, not years.

Worth checking before it does, not after.

If you want a second pair of eyes on how agent-ready your catalog actually is, that's a conversation I'm happy to have — comment or DM.
